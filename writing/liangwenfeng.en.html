!We are very willing to help and assist anyone, even our competitors——封面取自「不争」所在局部

Summary — 并读 Dean Ball、阿莫迪与梁文锋三场表态，看见三种 AI 世界观，落回梁文锋的『不争』。

Last night, "elsewhere" published the much-discussed remarks Liang Wenfeng made during DeepSeek's four-hour investor meeting. It is now the most widely circulated piece of reporting on a live, flesh-and-blood Liang Wenfeng anywhere online.

The author, Liu Jing, was previously editor-in-chief of "Waves+", the in-depth vertical under 36Kr. After leaving 36Kr he struck out on his own and founded the independent content project "elsewhere" ("Happening Elsewhere+"). Today Tencent Tech has also put online its own transcript of the nearly four-hour session it obtained, sorted and categorized into 118 items in all.

Faced with a transcript this seamless, outside doubt is inevitable. Some sift the source; some knock at the plausibility of the content — plausibility, after all, is sometimes only a means of persuasion. Others read it as nothing more than a PR push ahead of an IPO.

Even if this really was a strategic statement delivered in public, what came to mind the moment I read it was two other statements — Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's interview with Bloomberg, and OpenAI's head of strategy Dean W. Ball's public remarks on Kimi K3's open-sourcing.

Behind these three statements, each speaking for a different company's worldview, Liang Wenfeng — measured against the prejudice coming out of the West — is bracingly, refreshingly clear-eyed. That feeling I had to get off my chest.

Dean W. Ball

I cannot understand why the American tech world, which has spent so long posturing as the standard-bearer of justice, truth, goodness, beauty and the lighthouse, has grown so closed and narrow.

Well then. Arrogance and double standards have always been the mask Western elites never took off.

On Twitter he labeled open-source models "decelerationist" and went so far as to pin on them the hat of "Dystopian Hellscape." It has to be said — Americans are still, to this day, in love with vocabulary this saturated with Cold War color.

And then, brazenly, he advised the U.S. government to adopt rumor-mongering and dirty tricks.

Put plainly, it goes like this — have the Federal Reserve issue a report claiming Chinese models contain backdoors; that alone will create an appropriate amount of panic, and the claim needs no evidence.

(You just need to direct every agency to issue soft law that creates FUD. "A Federal Reserve Advisory Bulletin found that there may be backdoors in Chinese AI models." It needn't be that well justified. You just create enough regulatory risk that every regulated enterprise backs off.)

After being criticized by self-appointed righteous users on Twitter, he walked back his line on open source, distorting China's open-sourcing of its models as "forced export because the U.S. has it by the throat on compute" and as "the CCP's Strategic Blindness on AI" — the whole thing premised on the claim that China opening its models is unreasonable, abnormal behavior. He forces a negative lens onto the question and flatly refuses to admit that open source is a deliberate Chinese industrial strategy.

They cannot accept that Chinese engineers can do, with a fraction of the resources, what costs them astronomical sums of capital — and still less can they accept that China is using "open source" to tear down the West's "closed-source compute hegemony" outright.

In the end his anxiety reduces to a single sentence — the success of open-source models stands in the way of capital's windfall investment in Silicon Valley's closed models.

Dario Amodei

If Dean W. Ball's foolishness on social media is a display of loyalty to OpenAI's not-so-open strategy, then Dario shows what Western elites do best — sanctimonious double standards and hypocrisy.

Of the three, Dario's remarks carry the most "philosophical depth" and "moral anxiety," but that does not make them good. Quite the opposite. Precisely because his discursive system is finer-grained and more self-consistent, the bias built into it is better concealed — wrapped in a sugar coating of "it is all for humanity."

He begins from "risk to human civilization" but lands on "America first" national security. He defines himself as a patriot and says plainly that he wants the so-called "democracies" to hold the power of AI. But when the host presses him sharply on whether Claude was involved in a deadly strike on a girls' school in Iran, his answers start to get slippery. First he cuts loose — "we don't know how the model gets used." Then he shifts responsibility onto military decision-makers — "policy has to be left to military decision-makers; all we can do is set high-level boundaries." Finally he excuses himself with "at least a human makes the final call."

When the grand narrative of "preventing civilizational collapse" cashes out as concrete commercial contracts from the U.S. military, his compassion for humanity as a whole quietly contracts into obedience to American interests. Yes — the man who "wouldn't release the Mythos model, for safety's sake," has handed humanity's firepower to AI.

A man who claims to manage "existential risk," faced with a case in which AI may already have killed more than 150 children, does not first press for the truth. His first move is to discuss where responsibility sits.

Responsibility belongs, of course, to those who can make military decisions. I am only a technology supplier; their "micro-errors" are not mine to manage.

When the risk is abstract and in the future, I am the guardian of human civilization; when the harm is concrete and in the present, I am only a supplier honoring a contract.

Liang Wenfeng

Set against these two Westerners — one openly attacking open source to protect a windfall barrier, the other masking double-standard hegemony in a mouthful of morality — there are many, many more double standards and biases I have not listed.

But the Liang Wenfeng in the media — the one where you cannot tell whether it is really Liang Wenfeng himself — leaves me admiring and pleased.

— I have cut out the lines that moved me — it really does read like a master of the hidden groves rebutting the arch-villain, which is why it feels so satisfying. Full of vision, hungry for long-termism, yet restrained — and so it also carries that supremely cocky air of "I don't care" and "I am not pretending."

"Because he does not contend, none under heaven can contend with him."

He simply says — we are a group of ordinary people trying to get this one thing done. We only earn a reasonable profit. We open-source our strongest models. We contend with no one.

I share in the glory. China's technologists — seek truth from facts, open and other-serving, confident and self-reliant. He wants to build AGI. He believes restraint and openness are the more effective path. That is all.
